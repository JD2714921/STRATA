## CMC contracts and current Silver metadata

The currently exposed `get_price_history` schema and fetched `cmc://tool/get_price_history` capability document support daily `ONE_DAY` and multiple intraday intervals. Date-only request bounds become start/end of day **UTC**. This is request-bound behaviour, **not a definition of bar anchoring**. The response has exact-string bid, ask and mid OHLC (`b`, `a`, `m`), source timestamp `t`, optional volume and a display `dateFormat`. Neither schema nor capability text specifies whether `t` is the opening or closing boundary, daily session cutoff, a closed/partial flag, historical session calendar or corporate/cash-adjustment policy. Those remain unresolved.

The existing `cmc-daily-research.json` was inspected without downloading it again: GBP/USD 727, Silver - Cash 723 and US NDAQ 100 - Cash 722 rows. All rows retain bid/ask/mid OHLC; none has a closed flag. Source timestamps span 2024-01-02T00:00:00Z through 2026-10-20T00:00:00Z. Thus historical bid/ask data are already present; the legitimate gap is their timestamp/execution interpretation and cost completeness, not absence of bid/ask columns. Mid-return correlations omit spread/carry. Separate bid/ask extrema do not identify simultaneous tick spreads or executable fills; no fill/slippage model was validated.

`get_products` supports `financialConfiguration`, `tradingHours`, IANA timezone, current/upcoming versus typical sessions and account-specific source economics. Two bounded Silver SB calls used the same `Europe/London` timezone and returned:

| Field | Observed result | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Current/upcoming sessions | Example 20 Oct 23:00 → 21 Oct 22:00; after 25 Oct, 22:00 → 21:00 | Source dated sessions are available, but they do not establish the historical 2024–2026 calendar |
| Typical sessions | Example 20 Oct 18:00 → 21 Oct 17:00 | Five-hour disagreement with the above for the same requested/returned timezone; no correction is inferred |
| Session timestamp representation | Local-looking ISO strings without explicit offsets; response timezone `Europe/London` | Preserve both fields; do not append `Z` or silently treat the strings as New York time |
| Holding costs | `not_applicable`; buy, sell and effective-at null | No historical carry series or numeric zero was supplied |
| Conversion | `available`; factor `158.73015873`, point multiplier 100, price decimals 3, quantity decimals 2; fraction/lot null | Current account metadata exists, but does not independently define historical values or final broker-unit reconciliation |

The public [CMC spread-betting holding-cost explanation](https://www.cmcmarkets.com/en-gb/spread-betting/holding-costs-explained) describes 5pm New York valuation, historical holding rates in the platform and cash-commodity price adjustments linked to futures. Consequently, the null/not-applicable metadata response cannot be promoted into a zero-carry backtest. The page also mixes EST and New York wording; use a verified IANA/calendar policy rather than a fixed UK-hour assumption. No exposed historical-carry endpoint was found in the reviewed schemas. Live-pricing handlers remain disabled in the current descriptions; the delayed path is labelled 15 minutes.

**Still needed from CMC/provider contract:** interval-specific open/close anchoring; partial-bar semantics; daily cutoff and DST; historical holidays/early closes/breaks; correction and cash-adjustment treatment; historical holding rates and valuation basis; bid/ask sampling and execution costs. The typical/upcoming session mismatch needs provider clarification or an independently verified contract before calendar-based filtering.

## Consensus and reproducibility gap

No verified archive of immediately pre-release, event-specific market consensus was obtained in this bounded research. The earlier Bigdata/FXStreet calendar snapshot contains provider consensus fields but lacks a proven historical vintage/cutoff/contributor contract. Official actual-release archives do not fill that expectations gap. SPF and HMT are legitimate **published survey/forecast** evidence at their own horizons; they must not be relabelled as event-day market consensus.

An event-study input must retain series and release IDs; reference period; units/seasonal-adjustment/annualisation basis; expected-value evidence type; forecast publication, submission/vintage and availability timestamps; release timestamp/timezone; first actual; separately versioned revisions; source bytes/hash; receipt time; and a verified bar window/calendar. Null means unavailable. No exact timestamp, holiday, fee, spread or consensus vintage should be inferred merely to make the join succeed.
